## I'm That Bearded IT Guy.

I'm Liam.

I work in IT, I like figuring out how things work, and I have a habit of starting projects just to see if I can make them work.

That Bearded IT Guy started as a simple idea: somewhere to put all of that stuff - the things I learn, the things I build, the problems I run into, the things I find interesting, and probably a few things that seemed like a good idea at the time.

It's grown into a place where I can document the practical side of IT and the things I'm learning along the way.

---

## How I think about IT

I've always been more interested in understanding something than just making it work.

If something breaks, the first question isn't always "how do I fix it?" Sometimes it's *why did it break?* And if it keeps happening, *why are we still seeing it?*

That doesn't mean everything needs a complicated solution - quite the opposite. Sometimes the best answer is a really simple one. But I like understanding what's happening before changing things, keeping a record of what I've done, and looking at whether there's a better way of doing it next time.

That's probably why I find things like troubleshooting, documentation, process improvement and service management interesting.

---

## Why TBITG exists

There's a lot of technical information out there. Some of it is brilliant. Some of it assumes you already know half the terminology.

What I want TBITG to be is a bit different - a place where I can share things from my own experience and projects in a way that makes sense. If I've solved a problem, I'll write about it. If I've built something, I'll show how I built it. If I've learned something useful, I'll share it. And if I get something completely wrong, there's a good chance I'll write about that too.

The point isn't to have all the answers. It's to keep learning and share what I find along the way.

---

## Built, broken, fixed.

That's basically how I learn. I like getting hands-on with things, which is one of the reasons I have a homelab - a place to experiment, test ideas, build things and occasionally discover that the thing I changed five minutes ago has broken something else.

It's also a good place to try things I don't necessarily get to work with every day: Proxmox, Docker, Linux, Raspberry Pi, self-hosted services, scripting, networking, and whatever else I happen to be interested in at the time.

Some projects are useful. Some are just experiments. Either way, I usually learn something.

---

## Outside the screen

I'm not always thinking about IT. I spend plenty of time outdoors, especially paddleboarding, getting out with the dogs and generally finding reasons to put the laptop down for a while.

I think that's important. Technology is a big part of what I do, but it doesn't have to be everything.

## One last thing

I'm still learning. There are plenty of things I know reasonably well, plenty of things I'm still getting better at, and plenty of things I haven't learned yet. I'm quite happy with that - IT changes too quickly to ever really be finished learning anyway.

So that's what TBITG is about.

**Build something. Break something. Figure out why. Fix it. Learn something.**

Then probably do it all again.
